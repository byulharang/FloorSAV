The idea

A shared map for what you see,
hear, and where things are.

A person can be heard without being seen. A nearby object may be far from your path. FloorSAV brings camera motion, sound locations, and object landmarks into one coordinate system—so an AV-LLM can reason across views.

Read the abstract +

While 3D spatial reasoning in dynamic egocentric environments is crucial for embodied intelligence, audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) lack explicit mechanisms to process and internalize global geometry directly from raw sensory streams. Existing approaches either require costly fine-tuning or underutilize the model’s cross-modal reasoning capacities. In this paper, we propose FloorSAV, a novel framework that explicitly grounds spatial audio-visual context by rendering a dynamic 2D floormap. By integrating 3D point clouds, camera trajectories, spatial audio cues, and semantically grounded object landmarks, we inject this floormap into the AV-LLM as a synchronized stream with an egocentric video. AV-LLMs utilize their multi-modal capabilities to jointly reason over visual, auditory, and geometric cues in a single inference with floormap interpretation guidance. We further introduce SAVED-Bench (Spatial Audio-Visual Egocentric Benchmark with Dynamic Agents), constructing essential tasks of spatial capability in real-world scenarios: dynamic relativity, regional, and path reasoning QAs. FloorSAV improves AV-LLMs’ spatial reasoning on various tasks from both SAVED-Bench and SAVVY-Bench. Studies with ground-truth floormaps demonstrate the substantial potential of FloorSAV with accurate spatial information.

Evaluation details and release version +

Source: saved1988_ego2exo_out_set3_v1_20260925. FloorSAV corresponds to Map-task; oracle columns correspond to Partial-task and Full-task. Partial oracle supplies true other-person positions; full oracle additionally supplies ground-truth object labels. These are separate oracle conditions.

The current release combines 1,888 existing responses with 100 replacement Ego-to-Exo Direction FoV-out responses. The replacement uses a larger output-token budget. This is a combined release, not a fresh evaluation of every question under one setting. The CSV provides full-precision scores; manuscript figures may round values or report a different release.

All category averages improve; Exo-to-Ego Distance decreases from 46.40 to 44.65. Gains shown here are differences between the reported scores.

Looking ahead

A map is a starting point.

Sound localization errors, long video contexts, and reading map coordinates remain challenging. More accurate geometry can help—but robust spatial reasoning still requires progress across perception and inference.
